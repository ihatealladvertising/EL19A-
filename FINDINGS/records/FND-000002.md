# FND-000002 — F3D6/F3DE: two bounded UBYTE limit schedules with target-side mechanics

**Status:** CURRENT  
**Semantic layer:** function identity at the generic limit-schedule level; physical identity unresolved.

## Addresses and geometry

- Data `0x0F3D6` and `0x0F3DE`: separate 8×1 UBYTE curves, each `FF FF 43 43 A8 C4 E0 FF`.
- Axes `0x0F3C6` and `0x0F3CE`: separate 8×1 UBYTE objects, each `00 01 02 1C 58 68 70 80`.
- Reader: `sub_22EFC`; callers around `0x65848` (F3D6) and `0x68238` (F3DE).
- Query: `q = min(UWORD[FFFF85CE] >> 4, 255)` via `FFFF85D1 → FFFF85A9` and the block copy from `FFFF85C4` to `FFFF859C`.

## Observation

Both paths are selected by `FFFF99E7.bit3` set; clear bypasses the table path. F3D6 multiplies the interpolant by 13 and clamps between `4×UWORD[FFFF9866]` and `4×UWORD[FFFF9868]`, then stages at `FFFF2150+0xA88 = FFFF2BD8`; upstream producers of those two bound words were not established. F3DE multiplies by 13 and clamps to `908..2908`, derived from ROM UWORDs `0x1172C=0x00E3` and `0x1172E=0x02D7` multiplied by four, then stages at `FFFF2150+0xA8A = FFFF2BDA`.

Later processing writes `FFFF33EA` (F3D6) and `FFFF2C92` (F3DE). `sub_810A0` computes the absolute difference; `sub_810BC` uses `low_byte(diff) != 0` and `diff > 0x5C` with internal status `FFFF39A7` before calling generic dispatcher `sub_71F74` with event/service argument `0x4E`. No hardware register, actuator driver or external throttle command is reached.

Target code does not execute the donor `raw × 0.3575°` or `axis × 25 RPM` conversions. Additional upstream gates include `FFFF1E6E` bits and `FFFF3614/FFFF860B` state; these are target gates, not donor operating-mode labels.

## Conclusion

Retain `0xF3D6/F3C6` and `0xF3DE/F3CE` as two distinct, byte-identical-in-target 8-point UBYTE schedule pairs with different clamp regimes and staged outputs. Donor `mTTVOULM/mTTVOULMS` is a strong structural/arithmetic analogue only; do not promote donor degree/RPM units, main/sub labels or TVO physical identity into EL19A definitions.
