## 1. Objective and result

For the multi-byte (`size=2` or `size=4`) rows in `EVIDENCE/EL19A_scalar_alignment.tsv`, decide whether inferred EL19A addresses, target bytes and A2L storage declarations agree under the declared width and big-endian byte order, or are coordinate/scoring artifacts.

**Result: HYPOTHESIS SUPPORTED — `mixed`.** Of 671 multi-byte rows, 131 are `exact-big-endian` (target span bytes equal the pinned Juke reconstructed-ROM bytes at the Juke A2L address, with compatible big-endian A2L layouts), 204 are `byte-mismatch`, 336 are `unresolved` (Juke symbol absent, so no donor ROM span is claimed), and **0** are `byte-reversal-only`. A real byte-order-consistent subset exists, but it is outnumbered by mismatch/unresolved rows; little-endian reversal never rescues a mismatch. Structural/address confidence only advanced; no semantic promotion.

## 2. Falsifiable hypothesis record

- **Identity layer advanced:** structural/address confidence only; input, function, output and physical identity were not advanced.
- **Hypothesis:** a small subset of multi-byte rows is byte-order-consistent with donor and target storage; the remainder reflects byte-sequence alignment ambiguity or coordinate mixing rather than usable scalar correspondences.
- **Evidence:** WO65815 reproduced the committed TSV tier counts and anchor rows but not the reported global mapping count; WO65213 validated Juke A2L address-to-reconstructed-ROM mapping; WO65319 keeps donor name/unit out of EL19A. The TSV contains 68 `T1` and 171 `T2` two-byte rows plus 20 four-byte rows, with value columns that are numeric spans rather than uniform single bytes.
- **Prediction:** exact structural support requires the target span's bytes to match the donor span under declared width/endianness and an A2L declaration with compatible storage metadata. Byte-order reversal alone is not support.
- **Cheap discriminator:** bounded row-by-row comparison of only the multi-byte TSV rows against pinned ROM bytes and A2L declarations; no new alignment run and no broad ROM scan.
- **Result:** **SUPPORTED — `mixed`** (131 exact, 204 mismatch, 336 unresolved, 0 reversal-only).
- **Information gain:** separates a trustworthy multi-byte structural subset from width/endian artifacts and coordinate-mixed rows without reopening the global alignment question or another lane's agreement reconciliation.

## 4. Method and structural checks

- The TSV has **2,696 data rows**. Three descriptions are physically wrapped across two lines (`mRO2DMXF1`, `mRO2DMXF2`, `mRO2STINF`); they were rejoined read-only before parsing. Tier totals are T1 842, T2 708, T3 1,146, matching WO65815.
- Multi-byte rows total **671**: 651 at width 2 (68 T1, 171 T2, 412 T3) and 20 at width 4 (3 T2, 17 T3). No wrapped row is multi-byte.
- For every one of the 671 rows, the TSV `donor_addr` equals the V6 A2L address of the named symbol (**671/671**), confirming the TSV's V6-coordinate column.
- Juke A2L declarations were resolved for **335/671** rows (all `CHARACTERISTIC`/`REC_*` objects): `REC_UWORD_C` 265, `REC_SWORD_C` 58, `REC_ULONG_C` 7, `REC_SLONG_C` 5. These are the big-endian multi-byte layouts validated in WO65213. The remaining **336** rows are V6-only names in this comparison; no Juke ROM value was inferred for them.
- The XML fragment contains 1,550 tables (T1 842 + T2 708, T3 excluded). Its 242 multi-byte declarations are all `storagetype="uint16" endian="big"`: 239 at width 2 and **3 at width 4** (`mIGARN4`, `mCCEPMTHR`, `mILIMIT`), which is a width-declaration inconsistency. None of those 3 rows is an exact match.
- The TSV `el19a_value` equals the big-endian decoded EL19A span in **658/671** rows; `donor_value` equals the big-endian Juke span in **177/335** Juke-resolvable rows (118 exact and 59 mismatch). This confirms WO65815 §7's coordinate/provenance-mixing warning at multi-byte width: `donor_value` is not a uniformly established Juke byte/span.
- Exact matches are not only zero spans: 21 of the 131 exact rows are `00 00`/`00 00`; the other 110 have at least one nonzero byte and 77 have a nonzero high byte.
