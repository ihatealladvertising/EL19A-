## Question

## Answer

The originally cited ID `REC-000011` was already allocated to WO65808's initializer/publisher correction, so the record takes the next free IDs.

## What was published

- **`FINDINGS/records/FND-000012.md`** — "A GBR-relative displacement is not an address until the routine's own base is resolved". Carries the seven-step resolution procedure (nearest preceding `rts` as boundary; first `ldc rX,gbr` after it; pool-literal resolution; `mov.b`/`mov.w`/`mov.l` displacement scaling; `mov.w` sign extension; alternate mid-function entries that re-install a base; `lds.l @r15+,gbr` restores; constant propagation for `@(rN,gbr)` index registers), the six worked instances with their base sources, and the cross-link to `REC-000010`'s indexed-store blind spot.
- **`FINDINGS/reconciliations/REC-000013.md`** — four corrections, each with old claim, corrected claim, discriminating evidence and confidence:
  1. `0x0A19B8` reads `FFFF34E7` under base `FFFF34B3`, not `FFFF34E4` under `FFFF34B0` (WO65865).
  2. `FFFF109E` does not exist; the LPF staging byte is `FFFF8E9E` (WO65838/WO65859, corrected by WO65883).
  3. None of the eight `@(h'AA,gbr)` sites is `FFFF355D`; they are `FFFF10FE`, `FFFF36BE` and `FFFFB552`, so `FFFF355D`'s reader set is still exactly `0x07EB14`/`0x0935C2` (WO65886).
  4. The `FFFF34E7` bit0 clear is the dynamic-index store at `0x0A19EA`, and the byte is one shared cell across `FFFF34C1+0x26` and `FFFF34B3+0x34` with 18 access sites over five bits (WO65886, WO65892).
- **`FINDINGS/INDEX.md`** — additive only: revised `Index revision` line, one new address-lookup row covering `FFFF34E7`/`FFFF10FE`/`FFFF36BE`/`FFFFB552`/`FFFF355D`/`FFFF8E9E`, one new conflicts-resolved bullet, one new `FND-000012` row in the migration-coverage table, extended FND/REC range lines, and one completed priority-migration item.
