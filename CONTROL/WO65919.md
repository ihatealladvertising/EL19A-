## Question

WO65907 found exactly one access to `U16[FFFFB552]` — the 16-bit read at `0x032E8E` — and no store. Does a producer exist at all, or is the cell read-only and permanently zero?

## Why it has value

A payload byte that is always `0x00` makes the message it feeds constant, which is a materially different statement from "a live measurement is transmitted". Reading `0` as "unknown value" versus "no producer" is exactly the distinction `FND-000010` exists to prevent, so the answer decides whether any consumer of that five-byte message may rely on the field.

## Exact scope

1. Census stores to `FFFF B552`/`FFFF B553` across the `FFFFB4A8`-base function family: the functions that load `word_… ; h'FFFFB4A8` (at least `0x021250`, `0x023CAC`, `0x023D02`, `0x023F20`, `0x0288E8`, `0x02892C`, `0x02A38C`, `0x02A3E4`, `0x02A678`, `0x02B3AC`, `0x02DF82`, `0x02DF9C`, and the remainder of the list). For each, resolve whether any `mov.w`/`mov.b` store lands on `base+0xAA` or `base+0xAB`.
2. Check word-granular writes that would cover the cell from a different alignment (`@(h'A8,gbr)` word stores, `@(h'AA,gbr)`), and `mov.l` writes from a `FFFFB4A8`-derived pointer.
3. State the result as `PRODUCED` (naming the writer), `NEVER WRITTEN` (read-only zero), or `UNRESOLVED`.
4. If `NEVER WRITTEN`, say plainly that the message field is constant zero and that no consumer may treat it as a measurement.
