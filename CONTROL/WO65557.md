## Objective

Determine whether exact payload-and-axis fingerprints plus order-preserving piecewise alignment can reliably transfer **addresses only** between known ROM/definition pairs. Build and backtest one small, reproducible prototype before recommending any use on unknown EL19A scalars. This is not an automatic labeling or semantic-transfer tool.

## Phase 1 — trustworthy anchor set

Build a compact anchor table from the verified pair(s). An anchor is eligible only when all applicable evidence exists:

- exact ROM and definition provenance;
- exact payload bytes and extent;
- exact axis bytes/extent and axis direction/encoding where applicable;
- storage type and dimensions;
- unique or demonstrably discriminative fingerprint within the searched ROM;
- independent confirmation that the definition address is correct.

Exclude flat, low-entropy, repeated/common-byte, truncated, ambiguous, or definition-only matches from positive anchor scoring. Keep rejected candidates with reason codes so false matches are measurable. Preserve source paths, hashes and offsets for every anchor.

For EL19A, reuse WO65323's AC62/ACA2 64/64 payload identity and donor-axis evidence only at its stated scope; do not upgrade ACE2/AD22 or E424/F10E/F2B2/F426 without new exact evidence. Reuse WO65319's donor provenance correction. Reuse WO65551's constrained scalar-alignment result only where it independently survives. Keep address correspondence separate from calibration semantics.

## Phase 2 — bounded alignment prototype

Implement only these operations:

1. Fingerprint eligible payload-plus-axis anchors.
2. Match anchors between the two verified ROM/definition pairs.
3. Select an order-preserving anchor chain (LIS or equivalent), reporting ties and rejected crossings.
4. Segment the address map into piecewise regions only where anchor offsets support a stable local mapping. Leave insertions/deletions and unsupported gaps unmapped; never interpolate across a discontinuity without independent anchors on both sides.
5. For scalar candidates, use only valid, invertible conversion metadata and distinctive raw values. Unsupported conversions and unconstrained 0–255 ranges cannot count as positive evidence.
6. Emit candidate correspondences with explicit provenance, score components, region, anchor support and rejection reason. Never emit physical names, units or semantic labels.

Keep the implementation small and deterministic. No whole-ROM brute-force expansion beyond the selected verified pair(s), no machine-learning model, no external web/data expansion, and no automated XML editing.

## Phase 3 — independent backtest and metrics

Use a second known ROM/definition pair as a held-out backtest, not as training evidence for its own result. Where possible, hide known definition addresses, predict them from the remaining anchors, then compare predictions with the held-out definition.

Report:
- eligible and rejected anchor counts, with reasons;
- exact payload-and-axis match counts and duplicate/ambiguous fingerprint counts;
- anchor-chain length, ties/crossings, piecewise regions and unmapped gaps;
- scalar correspondence true positives, false positives, false negatives and unscorable cases against independently known addresses;
- precision, recall and false-match rate, with denominators and a clear statement if any metric is undefined due to insufficient ground truth;
- sensitivity to anchor removal and at least one null/chance-match control;
- examples of successful, failed and intentionally withheld mappings.

Do not call parameter stability, a plausible offset or a donor symbol match a true positive. Ground truth must be independent of the prediction.

## Falsifiable hypothesis

**Hypothesis:** Exact, discriminative payload-plus-axis anchors provide enough ordered constraints to recover some piecewise donor-to-target address correspondences without unacceptable false matches.

**Prediction:** On the held-out pair, the method predicts independently known addresses with measurable precision and recall, and the result degrades under anchor removal or null controls.

**Cheap discriminator:** Hide a subset of independently verified anchors/definitions, predict their addresses from the remaining anchors, and compare against exact held-out addresses. If the second verified pair or adequate ground truth is unavailable, the hypothesis remains untested.

**Information gain:** This separates a genuinely useful address-map method from accidental byte similarity, order assumptions and overfit offsets before unknown scalar candidates are exposed to it.
