## Objective

WO65487 proved the live `FFFF1640` writer (`0x6E20C`) stores a value that has been clamped by words at `r1+0xA1E`, `r1+0xA22`, `r1+0xA2C` and by `U16[FFFF3454+0x1C]`, but could not identify `r1`. Resolve `r1`'s base and the owning routine's entry, and classify what the clamp edges are (calibration limits, learned adaptations, or defaults).

## Mandatory falsifiable hypothesis

- **Identity layer advanced:** input identity.
- **Hypothesis:** `r1` is a RAM block base whose `+0xA1E`/`+0xA22`/`+0xA2C` words are *adaptation/learned-learn* limits rather than calibration constants, because the chain mixes them with `FFFF3454` (a work-area block) rather than with ROM addresses.
- **Evidence:** WO65487 §2 (`0x6E18A`–`0x6E1D2`), `FFFF1054` register-base convention, `FFFF3454` appearing as a work block.
- **Prediction:** the routine's prologue will load `r1` from a literal pool, and the resolved address will fall in RAM rather than ROM; a ROM copy of the same values would indicate calibration limits instead.
- **Cheap discriminator:** find the routine entry and prologue of the `0x6E1xx` writer, resolve its `r1` literal, then compare the `+0xA1E`/`+0xA22`/`+0xA2C` words against ROM.
- **Information gain:** decides whether `FFFF1640` is bounded by calibratable limits (nameable, exposeable in the definition) or by adaptive state (must stay hidden).
- **Result:** to be recorded on closeout.

## Scope

1. Locate the routine containing `0x6E1E6`/`0x6E20C` (search backwards for its `sts.l pr` prologue) and record its entry PC and any `r1` assignment.
2. Resolve `r1`'s literal base; compute the three absolute clamp addresses.
3. Classify each: RAM work area, adaptation block, or ROM-mirrored constant. Read at most three ROM bytes/words to compare.
4. State whether `FFFF1640`'s bound is calibratable or adaptive, with separate confidence.
5. Stop at an indirect dispatcher, a CAN/ADC read, or after the three clamp edges.
