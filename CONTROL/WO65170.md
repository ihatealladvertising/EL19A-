## 1. Decision answer

**No code path in this image consumes the records beginning at `0x13B50`.** The finding is a bounded negative, not a proof of non-existence: it is established by a complete 32-bit pointer census over the whole 1 MiB image, a corrected effective-address census of every base/GBR-relative access into the enclosing data block, and an instruction-level review of every consumer of `dword_13B44` (which reads only the +0/+4/+8 prefix).

Three independent lines of evidence converge:

1. **Zero pointer words.** Exhaustive scan of all 1,048,573 aligned-or-not 32-bit windows in `VERSA f501.bin`: only **two** words in the entire image hold an address inside `0x13B44–0x13BFF`. They are `off_21564` (`0x21564` → `0x13B44`) and `off_ABA14` (`0xABA14` → `0x13B4C` = `dword_13B44+8`). Both are prefix references. **No word anywhere points at `0x13B50–0x13BFF`.**
2. **No base- or GBR-relative access.** Every consumer that loads a base inside the enclosing pointer pool `0x13A00–0x13B44` (bases `0x13AE8`, `0x13AFC`, `0x13B00`, `0x13B10`, `0x13B18`, `0x13B3C`) reads at most `+0x34` from its base; the highest address reached is `0x13B1C`, i.e. `0x28` bytes **before** `0x13B44`. The four `dword_13B44` consumers read `+0` and `+4` only.
3. **The named peripheral registers are dead too.** Every ADC register address encoded in the block (`FFFFF819` ADCR0_B, `FFFFF818` ADCSR0_B, `FFFFF80E` ADDR7H_B, `FFFFF810` ADDR8H_B, `FFFFF814` ADDR10H_B, `FFFFF816` ADDR11H_B, `FFFFF839` ADCR1_B, `FFFFF838` ADCSR1_B, `FFFFF82E` ADDR19H_B … `FFFFF836` ADDR23H_B) appears in the listing **only at its `.res.b` declaration** — no instruction reads or writes any of them. By contrast the ADC2 group used by the analog chain *is* referenced in code (`ADCSR2_B` at `0x2546A`, `0x25882`; `ADCR2_B` at `0x2AAB8`).

Interpretation boundary: the block is unreferenced **static data** in this calibration. Whether an external tool, a different ROM build, or a hardware/DMA mechanism with no static pointer consumes it is outside this work order (see §8).

## 2. Extent, stride and field layout (structural, no semantics assigned)

`dword_13B44` is a single unlabeled `.data` run from `0x13B44` to `0x13BFF` (0xBC = 188 bytes) followed by the separate object `dword_13C00`.

| Range | Bytes | Content |
|---|---:|---|
| `0x13B44–0x13B47` | 4 | `0x00008204` (prefix +0) |
| `0x13B48–0x13B4B` | 4 | `0x000FFFFF` (prefix +4) |
| `0x13B4C–0x13B4F` | 4 | `0x00020004` (prefix +8) |
| `0x13B50–0x13BFF` | 176 | 11 records × 16 bytes |

Record stride is **16 bytes = four big-endian 32-bit words**. 176/16 = 11 exactly; 12- and 8-byte strides do not divide the 0x13C00 object boundary and do not reproduce the repeating field pattern, so 16 is the only stride consistent with the bytes.

Observable regularities (STRUCTURAL — instruction-independent, unit-free):

- `word0`/`word1` form two constant pairs, one per ADC block: (`ADCR0_B`,`ADCSR0_B`) for records 0–3, (`ADCR1_B`,`ADCSR1_B`) for records 4–10.
- `word2` carries a small value in the **most significant byte only** (`0x07`, `0x08`, `0x09`, `0x0A`, `0x0B`); the low three bytes are zero in all 11 records.
- `word3` is always an `ADDRnH_B` data-register address: for records 0–3 the value of `word2` equals the register index (`7→ADDR7H`, `8→ADDR8H`, `10→ADDR10H`, `11→ADDR11H`); for records 4–10 it maps `7→ADDR19H` … `11→ADDR23H`, i.e. index **+12**.
- Records 9 and 10 are exact duplicates of records 7 and 8. Only **9 of the 11 records are distinct**.

The `+12` offset in the second group is a real register-label fact (`ADDR19H_B` is `FFFFF82E`, i.e. file offset `0x2E` from `FFFFF800`), but it makes "word2 = channel number" and "word2 = data-register index" mutually inconsistent between the two groups. **No field semantics are promoted.** Any assertion that word2 is an ADC channel, gain code, or scan-list index is unproven without a consumer.

## 3. The block is one copy of a repeating build template

The same 11-record sequence is byte-identical in **eight** places in the image, grouped as five clusters (records detected by their constant header words):

| Cluster | Range | Records | Copies of the 11-record template |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | `0x13B50–0x13BFF` | 11 | 1 |
| 2 | `0x13C84–0x13E93` | 33 | 3 |
| 3 | `0x148B0–0x14A0F` | 22 | 2 |
| 4 | `0x14E24–0x14ED3` | 11 | 1 |
| 5 | `0x14F14–0x14FC3` | 11 | 1 |

88 records total, 11 per copy, 9 distinct records per copy, **zero byte differences between copies**. A per-variant descriptor table would normally differ between calibration slots; eight byte-identical copies is the signature of a padded/scaffold template emitted by the build, which is consistent with §1's finding that nothing reads it.

Pointer census across the whole family: the only 32-bit word that lands inside **any** cluster is the byte sequence `80 00 01 3D` at `0xC207C`, whose `0x13D01` is an unaligned sub-word of a `mov.w` constant pool (`h'8000` + `h'13D`, listing lines for `0xC207C/0xC207E`) and not a pointer. **No descriptor cluster in the family is referenced.**

## 5. Prefix semantics as observed (not promoted)

- **+0 `0x00008204`** — copied into `FFFF2404` by `loc_13B66`/`sub_21542` and used as the start pointer of a checksum walk that stops when `start+0x5E6C` or the +4 bound is reached. Reading it as "region start address" is supported only by that arithmetic.
- **+4 `0x000FFFFF`** — the walk bound in both `loc_21366` (`cmp/hs r11,r0`) and `sub_21406` (`add #1`, `cmp/eq`). Value is `2^20−1`, i.e. a top-of-image constant, not a region-specific end.
- **+8 `0x00020004`** — compared against `stc vbr` in the self-test at `0xABA04`. This is the only consumer that treats a prefix word as an expected value rather than a pointer.

The neighbouring `0x13B3C`/`0x13B40` pair (`0x0002040C` = `INT_ATU31_IMI3A`, `0x0002249E` = `off_2249E`) is a code-range bound used by `sub_21574`/`loc_215C6`. `0x13AA8` in the same block holds the ASCII `DATABASE` and `SH705822N`. The enclosing block is therefore code-integrity / build-identification data; the descriptor blob sits inside it without a consumer.

## 6. Corrections and caveats for reuse

**Erratum — `EVIDENCE/WO65117_effective_address_dictionary.tsv` double-scales displacement for `base_disp` and `gbr_disp` rows.** Its `disp` column already holds the byte offset, and its `effective` column adds `disp × 4` again. Verified examples against the listing labels:

| PC | Instruction | Listing target | Index `effective` | True EA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `0x2C688` | `mov.l @(off_13B1C - h'13AFC,gbr), r0` (`C6 08`, disp byte 8) | `off_13B1C` | `0x13B7C` | `0x13B1C` |
| `0x310DC` | `mov.l @(h'18,r7), r2` (`52 76`, disp nibble 6) | base `0x13AE8` | `0x13B48` | `0x13B00` |
| `0x310DE` | `mov.l @(h'34,r7), r6` (`56 7D`, disp nibble `0xD`) | base `0x13AE8` | `0x13BB8` | `0x13B1C` |
| `0x21380` | `mov.l @(4,r9), r11` (`5B 91`, disp 1) | base `0x13B44` | `0x13B54` | `0x13B48` |

Consequence: all 19 rows of that dictionary whose `effective` falls in `0x13B44–0x13BFF` are scaling artifacts. A naive index query returns four phantom accesses into the descriptor block. **Do not** use the dictionary's `effective` column for non-zero displacement without re-deriving the EA from the listing label. The index was still useful here — its `base` column and instruction text are correct.

**No-repeat list:** the `0x16CD0 → FFFFB61C` bulk copy, the `0x13B20/24/28` three-pointer bounds, `sub_24EA8`, `sub_2C574`'s message build, and the ADC2 analog chain were not re-traced. `dword_13B44` prefix consumer PCs were re-read only to confirm they stop at `+4`.

## 8. Stop boundary and next discriminator

Stopped at the intended boundary: no table pointer, runtime index or external consumer exists to follow. A dynamic-only consumer cannot be reached statically, and the named peripheral registers being untouched in code argues strongly against one.

**Highest-value next discriminator:** search the same 176-byte template (or its 9 distinct records) in a second EL19A-family image — another `VERSA f501` calibration revision, or the CF48D donor where permitted as a *donor-only* structural check. If the template is present verbatim regardless of calibration content, it is confirmed as build scaffold rather than an operational descriptor table; if it varies with the calibration, word2 becomes a calibration field worth re-opening with a consumer hunt in that build. Any CF48D comparison must stay donor-only and must not transfer addresses or semantics.

Secondary discriminator (cheap): check whether another calibration's ROM assigns a pointer to its own copy of the template. That would prove the mechanism exists in the toolchain even though this build has no consumer.
