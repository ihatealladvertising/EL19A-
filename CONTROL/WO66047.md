## Objective

Resolve two linked questions without presuming that the 32-point pair is MAF, airflow, engine load or RPM:

1. Does either \`INT_ATU02_ICI0A\` or \`INT_ATU12_IMI1AE\` supply the sample read as UDWORD \`FFFF14E0\` by \`sub_257AC\`? Establish the actual timer/channel, active clock configuration and MCU input-pin mapping only where target code and authoritative device documentation prove them.
2. Resolve the provenance of the forward-call operands \`FFFF1064\`, \`FFFF1066\` and \`FFFF10A0\` used by \`sub_648A6\` before the \`0x0F712/0x10F8A\` lookup. Distinguish previously computed software state from a direct measured input.

## Falsifiable hypotheses

- **H1:** One of the two ATU interrupt paths supplies or captures the UDWORD stored at \`FFFF14E0\`. Prediction: a direct register-to-cell, software-store, configured transfer, or proven pointer/descriptor edge connects a named channel to that cell.
- **H2:** The forward lookup operands are physical sensor/speed inputs. Prediction: their producer paths reach a named ADC/CAN/ATU input or a proven physical-unit conversion. A result of a prior lookup or internal arithmetic weakens this hypothesis.
- **H3:** The three operands have different origins. Prediction: one or more are prior lookup outputs/internal states while another remains an unresolved additive operand.

## Part A — capture interval source into FFFF14E0

### Reused dataflow

- \`sub_257AC\` reads current UDWORD \`FFFF1054+0x48C = FFFF14E0\` at PC \`0x257CC\`, and previous UDWORD \`FFFF8650\`. It forms an unsigned difference with its underflow correction, shifts right five and writes \`FFFF85E4\` at \`0x257E0\`. It reads \`FFFF14E0\` again at \`0x257EA\` to refresh the previous sample.
- \`sub_2088C\` calls \`sub_257AC\`. Its two established interrupt callers are \`INT_ATU02_ICI0A\` at \`0x25D5A\` and \`INT_ATU12_IMI1AE\` at \`0x26F46\`.
- WO65222's target-listing, effective-address, RAM byte/bit and transport-index checks found no confirmed software writer or configured DMA/DTC/block-copy producer for \`FFFF14E0\`. Its RAM-map area is reserved/undefined in that listing. The result is a bounded missing-producer finding, not proof that an unmodelled runtime or computed-pointer path is impossible.

### ATU02 / ICI0A result

- Vector \`0x20154\` enters \`INT_ATU02_ICI0A\` at \`0x25D5A\`.
- The known input-capture read is ICR0A at \`FFFFF434–FFFFF437\`; the handler stores that captured value to \`FFFF1990+0xD4 = FFFF1A64\` at \`0x25DA0\`.
- This is a real channel-0 capture path, but its observed destination is \`FFFF1A64\`, **not** \`FFFF14E0\`. The separate channel-0 \`TCNT0\` reads are used for execution-time deltas, not a demonstrated producer of \`FFFF14E0\`.
- The SH7058 device manual specifies the ATU-II channel-0 internal clock as \`φ/32\`; the ECU's actual \`φ\` and this path's relation to \`FFFF14E0\` remain unproven.

### ATU12 / IMI1E result

- Vector \`0x20194\` enters \`INT_ATU12_IMI1AE\` at \`0x26F46\`. The handler reads the channel-1 register block based at \`FFFFF440\`, then reaches the shared \`sub_2088C\` update path.
- Existing initialization evidence records writes of zero to \`TCR1A\`/\`TCR1B\` in \`sub_2982\` at \`0x2988/0x298C\`; \`OCR1=0x04E2\`, \`TSR1B\)/\`TCNT1B\) handling and \`TIER1B=1\` in \`loc_DD0\` at \`0xDDF0–0xDE02\`. These facts do not, by themselves, identify a running counter clock/prescaler, establish the actual active capture edge, or connect a capture value to \`FFFF14E0\`.
- The Renesas SH7058 Rev. 3 hardware manual lists ATU12 / IMI1E as vector 100 at vector-table offset \`0x190–0x193\`. The manual maps channel-1 inputs \`TIO1A–TIO1H\); \`TIO1E\` is the Port-D \`PD4/TIO1E\` function. For the documented packages, this is FP-256H pin 253 or BP-272 pin D2.
- This establishes a **hardware-capable candidate pin/function**, not the ECU's actual pin route. The target PFC selection for PD4/TIO1E, the fitted package, board routing and harness signal were not established by the reused target reports. No actual MCU input pin is therefore assigned to \`FFFF14E0\`.

### Part A conclusion

**Neither interrupt path is proven to supply the sample.** Both are proven execution triggers for the common \`sub_2088C → sub_257AC\` consumer. The known ATU02 ICR0A sample goes to \`FFFF1A64\`; the ATU12 IMI1E path reads channel-1 registers but has no demonstrated transfer to \`FFFF14E0\`. The \`FFFF14E0\` producer, timer channel, active timebase, capture edge and actual external pin remain unresolved. The \`48,000,000\` numerator in the later reciprocal arithmetic does not establish a 48-MHz clock or RPM.

## Part B — forward-query operand provenance

### Forward reader contract

At \`sub_648A6\`, ROM byte \`0x87C8.bit7\` selects UWORD \`FFFF1066\` when set or UWORD \`FFFF1064\` when clear. UWORD \`FFFF10A0\` is added, the result is UWORD-bounded, and the query is passed in R6 to \`sub_22FAC\` at \`0x648D8\` with values pointer \`0x10F8A\` and axis pointer \`0x0F712\`. The raw UWORD result is staged at \`FFFF10E2\` in the JSR delay slot. The selector is a ROM-bit branch; no D/N or operating-mode name is assigned.

### FFFF1066 — prior internal transfer result

WO65089's \`sub_68D94\` trace establishes a UWORD store to \`FFFF1054+0x12 = FFFF1066\` at the convergence point \`0x68F5E\`. The source is not a raw sensor read: preceding logic forms a query, selects/interpolates one of the two piecewise UWORD curves (\`0x10EEA/0x1100A\` or \`0x10F2A/0x1104A\`), and applies additional arithmetic/scaling using \`FFFF1054+0x384\`, ROM byte \`0x8F67\` and the first point of \`0x0F712\`. Thus \`FFFF1066\` is a computed internal UWORD state/result. Its ultimate physical input identity and units are not proven.

### FFFF1064 — prior lookup-result path, partial provenance

The reader-verified consumer crosswalk in \`INDEX/catalog_179_consumer_summary.tsv\` records \`0x0F712\` as read by \`sub_22FAC\` at \`0x69622\` in \`sub_69496\`, with a tainted UWORD result written to \`FFFF1064\` at \`0x69630\`. The same crosswalk records \`FFFF1064\` as subsequently read by \`sub_648A6\` at \`0x648BA\`. This is evidence that \`FFFF1064\` is a prior lookup result/state value, not sufficient evidence that it is a raw measured input.
  
The exact query producer and full instruction-level arithmetic feeding the \`0x69622\` call are not laid out in the cited WO65131 closeout and were not re-decoded from the local target listing in this coordinator pass. Accordingly, this edge is recorded as **index-derived / partial**, not as a fully reconstructed physical-input chain. The blanket no-writer statement in WO65098 is not safe to retain for \`FFFF1064\`.

### FFFF10A0 — additive operand, source unresolved

WO65089 proves that UWORD \`FFFF10A0\` is added to the selected \`FFFF1064/FFFF1066\` operand before the forward lookup. WO65098's bounded producer check did not identify a direct writer to \`FFFF10A0\`. No source, unit, sign interpretation beyond UWORD use, or physical identity is established. Retain it as an unresolved additive state operand; do not call it a MAF correction, RPM offset or sensor input.

### Part B conclusion

- \`FFFF1066\` is a computed internal lookup/calculation result (high confidence at the software-path level).
- \`FFFF1064\` has an index-recorded prior lookup-result path (partial confidence; its query producer remains unresolved).
- \`FFFF10A0\` is a UWORD addend whose producer/meaning remain unresolved within the cited bounded work.
- The forward query is therefore **not proven to be a direct MAF, airflow or RPM measurement**. The selector and additive operand do not justify those labels.

## Combined semantic decision

The pair \`0x0F712/0x10F8A\` remains a high-confidence, reader-verified, bidirectional 32-point UWORD transfer/lookup pair with separate caller paths and direction-specific post-scaling. Its physical domain and units are **unresolved**. The capture-derived connection through \`FFFF33EA\` is a software-calculated path whose timer source/timebase is not established. The provisional catalogue wording “MAF/Flow” remains a discovery hypothesis only; it is not promoted as a proven target calibration identity.
