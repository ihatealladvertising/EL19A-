## Question

Does `FFFF1DB4.bit5`, after being read in at least 6 routines, reach any second consumer beyond the internal state flag?

## Falsifiable hypotheses

1. `FFFF1DB4.bit5` is consumed by a named torque/throttle/dashpot/actuator output at a second consumer. **Result: NOT ESTABLISHED.**
2. `FFFF1DB4.bit5` is uncoupled from any consumer because the cell has no reader at all. **Result: SUPPORTED — stronger than WO65841's provisional reading.**

## Trace result

### The only `FFFF1D80`-based routine

`FFFF1D80` is loaded from exactly one pool literal (`off_36680`, verified at `0x036680`), used at `0x03651A` in the routine entered at `0x036518`. Its GBR-relative accesses are:

- `0x03651E` `mov.b @((byte_FFFF1DB7 - h'FFFF1D80),gbr)` → `FFFF1DB7`
- `0x036524` `mov.b @((byte_FFFF1D81 - h'FFFF1D80),gbr)` → `FFFF1D81`
- `0x0365CA`, `0x0365D8`, `0x0365DE` reads of `FFFF1DA7` / `FFFF1DB7`
- `0x0365A6`, `0x0365F2` stores to `FFFF1DB7` / `FFFF1D81`
- `0x036560`, `0x036566`, `0x036574`: `or.b #h'20, @(r0,gbr)` / `and.b #h'DF, @(r0,gbr)` with r0 = `h'27` in every case → `FFFF1DA7`, never offset `0x34`.

No access in this routine (or any other) reads or writes `FFFF1DB4`. The address appears in the listing only as its `.res.b 1` declaration; no pool literal encodes `FF FF 1D B4`, and no `@(h'34,gbr)` site executes with GBR = `FFFF1D80`.

## Verdict

`FFFF1DB4` has **no reader and no writer** in this image. `FFFF1DB4.bit5` therefore has no first consumer, no second consumer and no named endpoint — the question is closed by absence, not by a downstream trace.

**Classification: NO READER / NO WRITER (bit-level question moot).** No calibration interpretation attaches to this cell; it must not be given a definition or name.
