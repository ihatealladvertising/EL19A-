## Question

`0x078C0A` builds a four-bit mask from `FFFF3579.bit0` and `r12` bits 7/6/5 (values `1`, `2`, `4`, `8`) and stores it to `FFFF2150+0x6FE` = **`FFFF284E`**, gated by `FFFF34E2.bit4`. Does anything read `FFFF284E`, and does that read reach a named output — a diagnostic byte, a lamp, a PWM/actuator command — or does the mask terminate as internal state?

## Why it has value

`FFFF3579.bit4` (WO65910) terminated as internal state with no consumer. Bit0 is the opposite case: it is consumed and republished into a four-bit mask in the `FFFF2150` block. That mask is the first candidate in this lane for a value with a readable endpoint, and the `FFFF2150` block is already known (WO65913, `FND-000010`) to hold firmware-initialised limit fields — so establishing whether `FFFF284E` is a status byte with consumers or another dead store decides whether the whole `FFFF34xx`/`FFFF35xx` status network has any output at all.

## Exact scope

1. Enumerate every access to `FFFF284E` under each of its bases: `FFFF2150`-based `off_` loads (the site already uses `off_78E24`), `mov.w word_78CF0 = h'6FE`, plus any fixed-displacement or symbol-form site. Note base/offset per site.
2. Follow the first meaningful consumer: what the read selects and where that selection goes.
3. State the terminal: a diagnostic/publication byte, a lamp or relay, an MMIO/actuator store, another status byte, or `UNRESOLVED`.
4. Classify: `NAMED OUTPUT`, `INTERNAL STATE FLAG` (terminal named), `DEAD STORE`, or `UNRESOLVED`.

## Stop boundary

One byte, its access census and at most two consumer hops. No analysis of `FFFF3579`'s other bits (closed by WO65910), no re-trace of the six conjuncts, no `FFFF2150` limit-band work beyond what WO65913 covers, no vehicle testing, no XML or definition generation.
