# WO65045 — coolant-variable provenance and dashpot-threshold linkage

**Status:** ACTIVE  
**Priority:** targeted semantic investigation; bounded  
**Target:** `VERSA f501.bin`, 1 MiB, MD5 `46991697c502052752a2cabcb84c9e27`  
**Primary listing:** `EL19A_Complete_Disassembly_ANNOTATED_V3_NEXT6_16_ALL_PENDING_ANNOTATIONS.lst`

## Objective

Identify whether the candidate byte at `FFFF84BA+6 = FFFF84C0` is coolant temperature (or another sensor/state variable), then identify the actual comparison/condition that sets or clears the dashpot decision flag. Establish or reject a code-supported connection between the candidate sensor value, the threshold logic at `0x4043A–0x4044A`, and the dashpot test in `sub_403D8`.

## Starting evidence — provisional, reproduce against target files

- At `0x40422–0x4044A`, code loads UBYTE `[FFFF84B4+6] = FFFF84BA` into R5, conditionally adds 3 if `FFFF1DB4.bit5` is already set, loads ROM byte `0x8550 = 0x0A`, and uses unsigned `cmp/hs` to set/clear bit 5 of `FFFF1DB4`.
- The exact base/offset arithmetic must be checked carefully: the listing excerpt labels R2 as `FFFF84B4` and reads `@(6,r2)`, so the effective address is `FFFF84BA`, not `FFFF84C0`. The candidate is therefore **FFFF84BA**, with no +6 applied a second time.
- `sub_403D8` tests `FFFF860B.bit3` at `0x403EE`; this is the currently identified dashpot-related flag candidate.
- `FFFF1DB4.bit5` and `FFFF860B.bit3` are distinct fields. Their relationship is unproven.
- A prior bounded reader scan reported approximately 120–126 reads of `FFFF84BA`, but no direct writer surfaced in that scan. Counts differ between runs and must not be treated as authoritative.
- ROM `0x8550` contains raw byte `0x0A`; physical scaling/units are unknown.
- Do not infer coolant semantics from the threshold value, address proximity, reader count, or initialization-routine proximity alone.

## Required bounded work

1. Reproduce the effective-address calculation and full comparison/branch behavior at `0x40422–0x4044C`, including both hysteresis paths, flag polarity, and whether the compare result actually controls bit 5.
2. Identify the producer of UBYTE `FFFF84BA`:
   - Search the listing/XREF for direct writes, read-modify-write operations, block copies, table-driven sensor record updates, and indirect/alias writes within a narrow justified scope.
   - Inspect initialization of the relevant sensor/data block around `0x77608` only to establish layout and initialization behavior; do not equate zero-initialization with sensor identity.
   - If producer resolution crosses an alias, bulk-transfer, interrupt, or peripheral boundary, record the exact boundary and stop rather than launching a global RAM-writer scan.
3. Determine whether `FFFF84BA` belongs to a sensor block whose channel, conversion, or adjacent fields identify coolant temperature. Use corroborating code/data structure evidence; otherwise retain “sensor/state byte — identity unresolved.”
4. Trace all direct readers of `FFFF1DB4.bit5` within a bounded xref set. Establish what behavior it gates and whether it feeds any dashpot logic.
5. Trace writers/producers of `FFFF860B.bit3`, beginning with the known `sub_403D8` test and the previously reported direct writers. Establish the exact comparison/condition that sets/clears it, if present.
6. Test the linkage: does the coolant candidate or `FFFF1DB4.bit5` reach the `FFFF860B.bit3` producer through a direct data/control dependency? If not demonstrated, explicitly reject the proposed linkage for the examined path.
