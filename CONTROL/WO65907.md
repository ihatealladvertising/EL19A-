## Question

`0x032E8E` reads `U16[FFFFB552]` (`C5 55`, base `FFFFB4A8` from `sub_327FE`), zero-extends it into `r0`, copies it to `r7`, spills the low byte to the stack at `(h'5C+var_59,r15)`, and calls `sub_32790` at `0x032E9E` before `shlr8 r7` extracts the high byte. Nothing has established whether this 16-bit word is a calibration quantity, an internal counter, or a parameter destined for a named output. Where does it come from, and what does `sub_32790` do with it?

## Why it has value

`FFFFB4A8` is the base of one of the firmware's periodic timer bodies (WO65877 used its `+0x116` saturating counter), so this word may be a timer period or a counter rather than a command value. Either answer is useful: a timer/counter reading closes the `FFFFB552` line as internal timing state, while a command value with a downstream sink would be the first named-output lead found in the `sub_327FE` dispatcher.

## Exact scope

1. Establish what writes `U16[FFFF B54A–FFFFB552]`: own-base resolution for every `FFFFB4A8`-based writer of that span, including the dynamic `@(rN,gbr)` stores flagged by `REC-000010`/`FND-000012`.
2. Trace `sub_32790` (`0x032790`) far enough to state what it does with its two arguments (the 16-bit value in `r0`/`r7` and the stack-passed bytes): comparison, table index, arithmetic, or store. Bound it to the routine body and its immediate calls.
3. Follow the low/high byte split after `0x032E9E` (`shlr8 r7`, the `(h'1C,r15)` and `(h'24,r15)` stack slots set at `0x032E88`/`0x032E8C`) to their first meaningful use inside `sub_327FE`.
4. Classify: `NAMED OUTPUT`, `INTERNAL TIMING STATE`, `INTERNAL STATE FLAG`, or `UNRESOLVED`, with PCs.
