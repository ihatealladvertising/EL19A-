**Status:** ASSIGNED — WORKER 3 — READY → CLOSED — BOUNDED COMPLETE
**Priority:** P2
**Workstream:** STRUCTURAL / MULTI-CALL FUNCTION CORRESPONDENCE
**Owner:** Worker 3
**Branch:** `main`

**Closed:** 2026-11-26
**Dependencies:** `CONTROL/WO70006.md`; `CONTROL/WO65319.md`; `CONTROL/WO64830.md`; `CONTROL/WO65133.md`.

**Result:** `0x77616` is **not** a function entry or a call PC — it is the `sts.l pr,@-r15` instruction inside `sub_77608`'s register-save prologue (entry `0x77608`). The reported "six calls / five addresses" resolves as **six calls to six distinct addresses**: the address list dropped `0x103A4`, which is a separate 16-entry UWORD curve (`dword_102E4+0xC0`) read at its own callsite. The bounded function actually makes **20 ROM-byte references — 16 indexed table lookups plus 4 scalar threshold bytes** — so the reported six are a sub-cluster, not the function's lookup set. The three B-family objects are **16×16 UBYTE (256 B each), contiguous**, read by `sub_22DA8` with rows ← `0x0E0BE` and columns ← `0x0E0E2`. Donor one-to-one assignment is **not established**: `mTCREM` (4-point UWORD) and `mTDLMFD` (8-point UBYTE) have no 4- or 8-entry object anywhere among these 16 lookups.

## 1. What `0x77616` actually is

```text
sub_77608:
0x77608  2F 86        mov.l r8,@-r15
0x7760A  2F 96        mov.l r9,@-r15
0x7760C  2F A6        mov.l r10,@-r15
0x7760E  2F B6        mov.l r11,@-r15
0x77610  2F C6        mov.l r12,@-r15
0x77612  2F D6        mov.l r13,@-r15
0x77614  2F E6        mov.l r14,@-r15
0x77616  4F 22        sts.l pr,@-r15      <-- reported "function/caller 0x77616"
0x77618  4F 12        sts.l macl,@-r15
0x7761A  4F 13        stc.l gbr,@-r15
0x7761C  D0 9D        mov.l off_77894,r0 ; h'FFFF34C1
0x7761E  40 1E        ldc   r0,gbr
0x77620  7F B0        add   #h'FFFFFFB0,r15   ; -0x50 frame
```

The function's first instruction is `0x77608`; `0x77616` is the eighth instruction of the prologue. Any "one-to-one V6 function match at `0x77616`" is therefore anchored to a **register-save prologue offset**, not to a function boundary or a call site. That is a reusable matcher-verification rule: a matcher-reported target address must be re-checked against the listing for a `sub_`/`loc_` label before it is treated as a function or call PC.

Prologue/epilogue: `gbr` is installed from `off_77894` (`0xFFFF34C1`) at `0x7761C`–`0x7761E` before the frame is taken, so every `@(h'NN,gbr)` in the body resolves against `0xFFFF34C1`. Frame `-0x50`; `r8` is released at `0x79312` (`mov.l @r15+,r8`), which places the `rts` after `0x79312`; the unbounded tail past `0x7931E` was not traced.

Early-out edges inside the body: `0x77624`–`0x7762C` tests `UBYTE[0x12844+6].bit7` and tail-jumps to `loc_792FC`; `0x77630`–`0x77638` tests `UBYTE[FFFF3628+5].bit1` and branches to `loc_779AA`.

## 2. The reported six-call cluster — exact call matrix

`sub_22DA8` is the documented 16×16 UBYTE reader (RP-006): `r4` = map, `r5`/`r6` = the two axis bases, `r7` = first UBYTE query, second query on the stack, row stride `0x10`, result UBYTE. Its axis search is `sub_233CC` with start offset `0xF` (16-entry UBYTE axis) and its tail is `loc_2332C` with column count `0x10`, so **rows ← `r5` axis, columns ← `r6` axis, 16 rows × 16 columns**.

| # | PC | Helper | `r4` data | `r5` rows axis | `r6` cols axis | query (rows) | query (cols) | destination |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `0x77CAA` | `sub_22DA8` | `0x0B212` | `0x0E0BE` | `0x0E0E2` | `UBYTE[FFFF85A4]` | `UWORD[FFFF8494] >> 8` | `UBYTE[FFFF2870]` (`0xFFFF2150+0x720`) |
| 2 | `0x77CC8` | `sub_22DA8` | `0x0B012` | `0x0E0BE` | `0x0E0E2` | `UBYTE[FFFF85A4]` | `UWORD[FFFF8494] >> 8` | `UBYTE[FFFF2871]` (`0xFFFF2150+0x721`) |
| 3 | `0x77CE2` | `sub_22DA8` | `0x0B112` | `0x0E0BE` | `0x0E0E2` | `UBYTE[FFFF85A4]` | `UWORD[FFFF8494] >> 8` | `UBYTE[FFFF2871]` (`0xFFFF2150+0x721`) |
| 4 | `0x7804C` | `sub_22E44` | `0x1037C` | — | — | `0xFFFF84B4+4` clamped | — | `0xFFFF2150+0x244` (via `r8`) |
| 5 | `0x78058` | `sub_22E44` | `0x103A4` | — | — | same `r14` as #4 | — | `0xFFFF2150+0x246` (via `r8`) |
| 6 | `0x78238` | `sub_22DF8` | `0x0E64C` | — | — | `UBYTE[FFFF84B4+5]` | — | `r13` (0x7823C) |

Register evidence for the row/column queries of #1–#3: `0x77C90` `mov.w word_77D24,r6` (`0xFFFF8494`), `0x77C92` `mov.w word_77D26,r10` (`0xFFFF859C`), `0x77C94` `mov.w @r6,r2`, `0x77C98` `extu.w r2,r13`, `0x77C9C` `shlr8 r13`, `0x77CA4` `mov.b @(8,r10),r0` → `UBYTE[FFFF859C+8] = UBYTE[FFFF85A4]`; the column query is stored to the outgoing stack slot by `0x77CA2`/`0x77CBC`/`0x77CD6` `mov.b r0,@(h'78+var_75,r15)` and the row query reaches `r7` through the delay slot `extu.b r0,r7` at `0x77CAC`/`0x77CCA`/`0x77CE4`.

**Selector:** `0x77CB4` `mov.b @(h'FC,gbr),r0` (gbr = `0xFFFF34C1` → `UBYTE[FFFF35BD]`), `0x77CB6` `tst #2,r0`, `0x77CB8` `bf loc_77CD4`. Bit 1 **set** runs `0x0B012` (#2); bit 1 **clear** runs `0x0B112` (#3). `0x0B012` and `0x0B112` are therefore **mutually exclusive alternatives writing the same cell `FFFF2871`** — they are never both read in one pass. `0x0B212` (#1) is unconditional.

**#4/#5 query:** `0x7802C` `mov.w word_7815E,r14` (`0xFFFF84B4`), `0x7802E` `mov.b @(4,r14),r0`, `0x78030` `extu.b r0,r6`, `0x78032` `add #h'FFFFFFE0,r6` (`-0x20`), `0x78034`–`0x78038` clamp at 0 (`bt`/`mov #0`), `0x7803A` `shll8 r6`, `0x7803E` `shll r6` (net ×512), `0x78042`–`0x78046` saturate at `0xFFFF`. Both calls pass `extu.w r14,r5`. So one shared 16-bit query derived from `UBYTE[FFFF84B8]` (`0xFFFF84B4+4`, the coolant-temperature image) drives both curves; results land in `0xFFFF2150+0x244` and `+0x246` (`r8` = `0xFFFF2150` from `0x77E22`/`0x77E42`).

**#6:** `0x78230` `mov.w word_7826A,r3` (`0xFFFF84B4`), `0x78234` `mov.b @(5,r3),r0` → `UBYTE[FFFF84B9]`, delay slot `extu.b r0,r5`; result to `r13` at `0x7823C`.

## 3. Reconcile six calls against the reported five addresses

The reported EL19A set was `0x0B012`, `0x0B112`, `0x0B212`, `0x1037C`, `0x0E64C` — five unique addresses.

**The sixth call is `0x103A4`, and it is a distinct object, not a second read of `0x1037C`.**

- The pool words are `off_78270` = `dword_102E4+0x98` = `0x1037C` and `off_78278` = `dword_102E4+0xC0` = `0x103A4` (`0x78270`, `0x78278`, both verified as `00 01 03 7C` / `00 01 03 A4` in ROM).
- They are separate 32-byte curves: `0x1037C`–`0x1039B` = `0014 0014 0014 0014 FB06 F522 EB6B DE07 CD2C B91E A22C 88B2 6D16 3138 11E5 11E5`; `0x103A4`–`0x103C3` = `003C ×4, 003A ×8, 0164 ×3, 011A`. They do **not** share content and are **40 bytes apart**, not 32; the 8 bytes `0x1039C`–`0x103A3` (`11E5 11E5 11E5 0000`) sit between them and are **not claimed** by either read.

So there is no duplicate-address reuse to explain the mismatch. The correct statement is: **six calls, six distinct data objects.** The reported five-address list was one item short; the count mismatch was a reporting omission, not a single address serving two symbols.

**Why this matters for the donor pairing:** six donor symbols for six calls invites a one-to-one assignment, and the short address list made `0x1037C` look like it served two symbols. With six real objects the pairing must be argued object by object — and §6 shows two of the six symbols have no geometrically compatible object here.

So the lookup census is **exactly 16 with no unresolved member**; the three `r4` loads listed above and the one `r6` load are scalar threshold bytes in the `0x08xxx` calibration region (`00`, `00`, `00`, `FF` on this ROM), and two of the four gates are inert on stock data.

Reader contracts (all verified from code):

- `sub_22DA8` — 16×16 UBYTE, row stride `0x10`, axes via `sub_233CC` (16-entry UBYTE).
- `sub_22DF8` — `mov.w word_22E0A,r0` = `0xE0`; `cmp/hs` → on `q >= 0xE0` returns `UBYTE[table+7]` (`locret_22E06` delay slot `mov.b @(7,r4),r0`); else `loc_23508` UBYTE interpolation.
- `sub_22E10` — `word_22E22` = `0xF0`; on `q >= 0xF0` returns `UBYTE[table+15]` (`mov.b @(h'F,r4),r0`); else `loc_2350C` UBYTE interpolation.
- `sub_22E44` — `extu.w` query; `off_22E5C` = `0xC19C`; on `q >= 0xC19C` returns `UWORD[table+3]` (`mov.w @(6,r4),r0`); else `loc_234E4` UWORD interpolation (`loc_2351C` `swap.w r3,r3` then the UWORD branch `mov.w @r4` / `@(2,r4)`).

So **every object in this function is 16-entry or 16×16.** There is no 4-entry and no 8-entry object anywhere in the constellation.

## 6. Donor comparison (metadata only; not executed, not resolved)

`EVIDENCE/side project/64811000p2_m.a2l` declares all six symbols; `RFCC_2XHK19BDF1.a2l` declares the three maps (`mTMSATH` line 25968, `mTMSATA`/`mTMSATB` adjacent, `mTMFPC1` via curve list). All six carry `/begin FUNCTION_LIST KF7-500.26[17]` in the 64811 A2L — they belong to **one donor function**, which is consistent with a single target function performing many lookups.

| Donor symbol | Type | Storage | Points | Declared axis | Target objects of that geometry |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `mTMSATH` | MAP | `REC_UBYTE_C` | 16×16 | `COMP_0020` 16 pt (`mLNMIS`), `COMP_0001` 16 pt (`mLPMIS`) | `0x0B212`, `0x0B012`, `0x0B112` (3) |
| `mTMSATA` | MAP | `REC_UBYTE_C` | 16×16 | same two | same three |

| `mTMFPC1` | CURVE | `REC_UBYTE_C` | 16 | `FIX_AXIS` 16, 400–6400 | 13 of the 16-entry UBYTE curves |
| `mTCREM` | CURVE | `REC_UWORD_C` | **4** | `FIX_AXIS` 4, −20–40 | **none** — no 4-entry object exists here |
| `mTDLMFD` | CURVE | `REC_UBYTE_C` | **8** | `FIX_AXIS` 8, −40–100 | **none** — no 8-entry object exists here |

Two findings follow, and both weaken the reported 1:1:

1. **Geometry discriminates only partially.** Three 16×16 UBYTE maps with two 16-point axes is exactly the `mTMSATH`/`mTMSATA`/`mTMSATB` class, and one 16-point UBYTE curve matches `mTMFPC1`. But `mTCREM` is a **4-point UWORD** curve and `mTDLMFD` is an **8-point UBYTE** curve, and this function contains no 4-entry and no 8-entry table at all. Those two symbols cannot be assigned to any object here, so a six-for-six mapping is impossible on geometry alone.
2. **`0x1037C`/`0x103A4` are 16-point UWORD curves**, which is a *UWORD* class. `mTCREM` is the only donor UWORD symbol in the set, but it has 4 points, not 16. Assigning either target curve to `mTCREM` would contradict its declared point count.

Per the V6 A2L↔HEX caveat (WO65212) and this WO's prohibitions, no symbol is assigned to any object, no donor unit or conversion is transferred, and the names `mTMSATH`/`mTMSATA`/`mTMSATB`/`mTMFPC1`/`mTCREM`/`mTDLMFD` are **not** attached to EL19A objects. The matcher aggregate (103/217, 45-consensus, 18 shared scalars) is not reproduced or relied on; its source and raw pair output remain absent from `main`.

The 18 shared scalar constants were **not** fingerprinted: only the count was supplied, and the WO requires exact addresses and values before any representation claim.

## 7. Relationship to WO65319 and the B-family interpretation

WO65319's target-side endpoint boundary is reused unchanged: no degree-decode, retard or knock-window endpoint is demonstrated for `0x9B2E`/`0x9C2E`/`0x9DAE`, and no downstream endpoint search was repeated here. Nothing in the reconstructed call matrix contradicts that boundary — the B-family outputs are numeric UBYTE/UWORD values that feed coefficient-weighted arithmetic (`sub_223FC` with RAM coefficients `0x8880`, `0x8881`–`0x8884`, and `0x887A`/`0x887C`/`0x887F`), not a retard accumulator or a named actuator.

The objects are **not renamed**. They remain generic raw 16×16 UBYTE surfaces and generic raw curves. Their orientation (rows ← `0x0E0BE`) and the mutual exclusivity of `0x0B012`/`0x0B112` are structural facts; no physical identity is claimed.

## 8. Confidence and limits

**High:** `0x77616` is a prologue instruction inside `sub_77608`; the six reported callsites with all arguments; the six data objects, their extents, storage and geometry; the row/column role of `0x0E0BE`/`0x0E0E2`; the four reader contracts; the `0x0B012`/`0x0B112` selector and shared destination; that the reported five-address list is one short; the absence of any 4- or 8-entry object in the function.

**Medium:** the tail of the function past `0x7931E` was not traced, and the purpose of each threshold gate is structural only (which comparison it guards, not what the comparison means).

**Unresolved:** physical identity and units of every object; whether `0x0B012` vs `0x0B112` is a variant/calibration selector; the meaning of the `0x1037C`/`0x103A4` pair and of the 8 unclaimed bytes between them; donor symbol assignment.

**Do not repeat:** the `sub_77608` prologue/`0x77616` determination; the six-row call matrix and its registers; the contiguous 256-byte B-family extents and the `0x0B212`/`0x0B112`/`0x0B012` ordering; the axis values and roles; the `0x103A4` omission; the four reader contracts; the donor point-count mismatch.

## 8a. Post-publication refinement (same session, after first publication as `cf4aecc`)

The three `r4` loads recorded in §5 as "not resolved to a consuming reader" were traced after publication. They are **not table pointers at all**: the code dereferences element `[0]` only (`mov.b @r4,r2`) and compares it, with no index computation and no reader call. A fourth such load uses `r6` (`0x784A6`, `UBYTE[0x08E37]`). ROM values are `0x00`, `0x00`, `0x00` and `0xFF`. The lookup count is therefore exactly 16 with no unresolved member, and the earlier "19 distinct addresses / 16 identified" framing is superseded by "20 ROM-byte references: 16 indexed lookups and 4 scalar threshold bytes".
