Determine whether the cross-payload delta bias found at periods 64/32 is a harmonic/longer-period phenomenon or a bounded short-period anomaly, using only the delta stream and the three recorded column statistics.

**Result: HYPOTHESIS SUPPORTED at the aggregate statistical layer, with a non-monotonic qualification.** Three of the six test periods (128, 192, 256) have IoC excess at or above the WO65605 period-64 reference, and period 256 is the clear maximum at `+1.543e-4` (2.076× the reference). ## Reference comparison and interpretation

- **Grows:** periods 128, 192 and 256 exceed the p64 IoC reference; period 256 is 2.076× on IoC, 2.957× on entropy deficit and 1.669× on max-frequency excess. Period 192 is essentially at the IoC reference but nearly double the entropy deficit.
- **Persists:** periods 96, 160 and 320 remain below the p64 IoC reference but still far above the within-payload screens, with larger entropy deficits than p64. Period 320 is close to the IoC reference (0.936×) and well above it on the other two statistics.
- **Changes ranking:** the strongest tested period shifts from 64 in WO65605 to 256 here. The six-period order is not strictly ordered by period size or by multiples of 32/64; period 160 is the weakest and period 320 is below periods 128/192/256 despite being a larger multiple of 64.
- **Concordance:** five of six periods move in the same direction as the prediction on all three statistics. Period 96 is the sole discordant row: entropy deficit rises while IoC and max-frequency excess fall slightly relative to p64.

This is a bounded statistical observation on the additive delta stream. It neither identifies nor excludes any cipher, compression or transport mechanism; it does not revive the failed MFB-delta decode, and it assigns no meaning to the payload bytes.
